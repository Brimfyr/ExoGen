# ADR 0010: A cap's outline by its own traps on the finer grid

Status: accepted, 2026-11-23. Supersedes ADR 0009's last consequence (a cap's outline its places' smooth contour on the climate's grid).

## Context

The caps an ice lays (F241) cover the coldest of its traps until they hold its inventory, each place's trap read smoothly between the climate's cells, some 170 km apart on Mars. The user found Mars's perennial caps drawn as solid shapes on the albedo map (2026-11-22), and chose, of the ways offered, to have their outlines placed by their own mass balance on the finer grid, in a milestone of their own (M102). Measured on the drafts before it:

- Mars's water gathered through its age (F259) piled, without flowing, 9 to 16 km deep on 12 of the climate's places at 84 to 88 degrees north, its whole 25 m there; the caps covered the places it gathered at the most first, read by the monotone cubic flat across that block of places, and where their ranks read alike the cut ran along the grid's rows, a rectangle in the north cap.
- The rest of the caps, four fifths of their places, followed the present season's traps, not the table the ice had gathered under: their coldest in the deep craters at 75 degrees north, so that the north cap was two craters filled with ice joined by the rectangle, and the south pole held no water.
- Pluto's and Triton's ices alike: blocks of the climate's places where they gathered, then the present traps' coldest, basins' floors at the climate's scale.
- Placing a cap's margin by the vapour its ice holds on each cell's own ground against its place's, by the cell's peak warmth (F423, tried in M101), let a few kelvin of the ground's own relief outrank Mars's near-flat polar traps, and scattered the cap.

## Decision

1. Each cell's trap is its own on the finer grid (F427): its ice's table on the latitudes' rows, an ice the orbit's cycles move the one its exchange answers them by (F259; a slow ice's the turning's mean), else the traps' own table where the climate's cells run on rows, read at the height above the datum of the ground's shape the grid resolves (F423), the caps already laid on it, and at the latitude that shape's slope faces, by the share of the sunlight it faces its temperature keeps (F237). Where each of the climate's cells runs its own traps, theirs are read smoothly at the cell and at the point it faces.
2. An ice covers the fewest coldest of those cells whose plastic profile holds its inventory, as before (F241, F418). The ices are no longer gathered through the body's age: without flowing their masses stood many kilometres deep on a few of the climate's places, and the caps their places' order drew were the blocks of those places. Each ice's table as its exchange answers the orbit's cycles, found before the gathering ran, places its caps, and the gathering is dropped.
3. The table an ice is read from reaches the finer grid's ground: its levels span the lowest and the highest of it, with the headroom of the caps laid on it (TRAP_HEADROOM); read beyond its levels a trap stood at the last level's, every deeper cell alike, and the cut among them ran by the cells' order again. An assertion holds it.
4. A slope's own sunlight moves its trap by the latitude it faces, a degree or two, so that where an ice's traps are near flat its slopes move its margin little: read by each cell's own peak warmth, they outranked the traps.

## Consequences

- A cap's outline follows the terrain at every scale the grid resolves: its margin crosses the ground where its own trap at its own height crosses the cut, filling basins and craters beside it, and it holds no lone cell where a cell's own relief stood on the other side of the cut.
- Mars's water lies at both poles, a cap at each and crater caps beside them, four fifths of it in the north. Its carbon dioxide's residual cap lies on the north's water, where its table's growth is the greater, which the gathered water's 16 km pile had kept it from: not the seed's ground but the water's own cap laid first at the north pole, as bright as clean ice; on flat ground without caps the present orbit favours the south, as on Mars. Mars's own lies in the south, its frost kept bright by how it is laid under the flow over Hellas and Argyre (Colaprete et al. 2005), a frost's brightness by where it falls that the model does not have.
- Where an ice's traps fall steeply with depth, as Pluto's and Triton's nitrogen and methane do below a few kilometres, its caps lie in the deepest ground the grid resolves, troughs' floors among it; and where they are near flat across a band of latitude, as Pluto's methane's, its caps lie in patches over the band where the ground's height crosses the cut. Both the model's own traps on the finer grid; what would gather such patches together (the ices' own albedo on their traps, their flow under their own weight) is not yet modelled.
- The gathering's run through the body's age, some 50 s of a Mars draft, is gone, the tables it was run on kept (106 s); its masses, the bands of latitude they held, are no longer reported.
- The working liquid's caps (the Earth-like's, TRAPPIST-1e's) cover all the ground their own warmth lets them (F282): on the drafts every map of theirs is byte for byte as before.
