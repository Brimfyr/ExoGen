# Phase 3 report: interior, volcanism, stagnant and episodic lid

Status: complete, 2026-10-11; reviewed twice the same day and revised after each review (the sections at the end). Awaiting the next look before Phase 4 starts.

Phase 3 gives every airless rocky world its interior history and lets that history shape the surface: cooling shrinks the body into thrust scarps, magma floods the basins and builds rises, shields and canyons, a foundering lid renews a whole surface, and a tidally heated moon resurfaces itself faster than impacts can mark it. Eight milestones went in, each committed to main on its own: the parallel finishing pass, the thermal history, contraction scarps, flood volcanism, lava subsidence with wrinkle ridges, plume rises, the episodic lid, and the Io regime. The finishing pass that Phase 2 built, coarse evolution plus resolved detail, carries all of it: every new process runs inside the loop at the step it happens and is sharpened against the resolved terrain afterwards, in time order with the craters.

## Gate results

The plan's gate was that the Mars preset produces a rise, a canyon system and giant shields consistent with the relief limit, that the Venus preset's crater population is sparse and uniform, and that the Io preset has no craters and hundreds of paterae. All three hold: Mars's tallest shield reaches 25.0 km under its relief limit; Venus carries 25 craters above the recording size, fresh, spread evenly and none older than the event; Io has 403 paterae and no crater. Each gate below is a test in `crates/exogen-core/src/stages`, run at preview quality on the reference presets; the full suite is 87 tests and `exogen validate` passes its 104 reference checks.

- **Mercury takes up its contraction in scarps.** The scarps stamped in the loop carry more than 30% of the shortening budget 2 ΔR / R × area (the rest is in scarps shorter than the loop grid), the longest exceeds 300 km and the highest 1.5 km, and the Moon has far less shortening per area. At standard quality Mercury carries 2,272 scarps, the longest 843 km, from 5.7 km of radial contraction (Byrne et al. 2014 give 5 to 7 km).
- **The Moon floods its thin-crust basins.** Lava covers between 5 and 35% of the surface, most of it on the thin-crust half, and the finishing pass keeps the loop's extent within 5%. At standard quality the maria cover 14.8% of the Moon (17% in reality) from 7,731 eruptions totalling 1.6e7 km³ (about 1e7 km³ in reality), with 1,561 wrinkle ridges on them.
- **Mars raises a rise with shields and a canyon.** The rise's crown stands more than 0.3 of its height above a ring beyond its foot (sampled many times each), the tallest shield more than half its height above its foot, the main canyon's floor more than 3 km below the plateau either side, and the Moon raises none. At standard quality: a 5,700 km rise 8.3 km high, five shields (the largest 684 km across and 25.0 km high, with two calderas), a rift population of some forty troughs the loop carries, the longest 4,700 km and 8.3 km deep, and eleven thousand graben.
- **Venus renews its surface and keeps tessera.** The event lies at the retention age, the tessera make up 4 to 14% of the surface and keep an older age, more than 70% of the plains carry the event's age, there are more than 50 coronae and some domes, and no crater is older than the event. At standard quality: tessera 7.9% standing as plateaus, plains 91.8%, 506 coronae of which some 150 are novae, 152 domes, three rises with nine shields, each with one or two rifts up to 3,000 km long and thousands of graben, 1,400 nova fractures, 78,000 wrinkle ridges, 765 flows since the event.
- **Io is craterless with paterae and mountains.** More than 100 paterae and 20 mountains, flow fields and plume rings, not one crater, fresh flows between 0.5 and 30% of the surface, and a mean colour that is yellow. At standard quality: 403 paterae (72 active), 149 mountains up to 15 km, 114 flow fields, 132 fallout rings of which two are the red rings of active Pele-class plumes; a gigayear before the present the surface carries a population of its own.
- **Unit gates.** The thermal history reproduces the present-day interior nodes within 2% and the young Moon is hotter with a thinner lithosphere; a scarp stamped on a flat grid steps up behind its face and tapers at its ends; a lava lake runs downhill, fills the pit and keeps its volume, and overflows a pass into the next pit; ridges laid out on a filled basin run along its contours at the planned length per area; depression filling levels pits and keeps slopes; the shield, trough, corona, dome, patera and block profiles have their shapes; the loop population still follows the production function with the steady-flux cap.

## Deliverables

- **Thermal history** (`thermal.rs`): heat flux, tidal flux from the eccentricity history, mantle temperature, lithosphere and elastic thickness, a relief limit that follows the elastic thickness, cumulative contraction and magma production, sampled through the loop and exposed as curve nodes.
- **Contraction scarps** (`stages/tectonics.rs`): a power-law population of lobate thrust scarps whose shortening equals the strain, each stamped at the step it forms so it cuts what is older and is cut by what is younger, with a 12° face, a long back slope and three harmonics of lobing along the trace; the finish restores the face. The longest scarp scales with the square of the strain, which gives Mercury thousand-kilometre ruptures and the Moon kilometre-scale ones below map resolution. The contraction law gained a 0.7 Gyr onset and a mantle term that scales down for bodies that accreted cool inside, so the Moon's record is a few hundred metres (Watters et al. 2010).
- **Flood volcanism** (`stages/volcanism.rs`): the extrusive share of the magma budget, cut back once the lithosphere passes 400 km or contraction closes the pathways, erupts from vents that favour thin crust and low ground; large impacts now thin the crust beneath them, so basins draw the eruptions. Each eruption fills one lake from the nearest low by priority fill, crossing passes into deeper ground, spreading no thinner than a flow and no further than 1,500 km, and subsiding by 5% of its thickness, which shows buried rims as ghost rings. Floods reset the surface age and unit; craters on lava take lava units. The finishing pass keeps a ledger of every flood's coarse smear and refills each flood to its level against the resolved ground inside a lobate outline of its footprint, so contacts are sharp and flow fronts lobate. Mare basalt and Mercury's brighter smooth plains join the materials.
- **Wrinkle ridges** (`stages/ridges.rs`): laid out at the end of the loop where the lava fill thins fastest and round a rise's load, following the thickness contours with inertia and wobble, a broad arch with a narrow wandering crest, standing 0.2 Gyr after the last flow they cross and cut by younger craters.
- **Plume rises** (`stages/plume.rs`): a dome grown over the first 1.5 Gyr that lifts what is on it, radial graben, a canyon system of overlapping scalloped troughs on a wide rise, and shields with 5° slopes capped by the strength-limited relief, with nested calderas and radial flow texture cut at the finish. Eruptions favour the rise.
- **Episodic lid** (`stages/episodic.rs`): the event fills every depression 70% of the way to its spill point and keeps the highest-standing 8% as tessera with a fabric of crossing ridges, refined at the finish against the resolved ground; afterwards a small share of the budget erupts, and coronae (annular ridges with concentric fractures round a dome or sag) and pancake domes dot the plains. Rises form on episodic lids too, narrower than on a thick stagnant lid.
- **Io regime** (`stages/io.rs`): on a rocky body above 0.5 W/m² of tidal flux the surface is laid down in the last step: paterae concentrated at low latitudes, a fifth active with black floors; dark flow fields with soft lobate margins; plume rings of red sulfur and white frost; elongated tilted mountain blocks with ridged texture; sulfur plains reddening towards the poles with patches of frost. Deposits and flows blend over the plains by a coverage carried in the melt field. No crater is kept.
- **Finishing pass**: parallel waves of non-overlapping stamps, byte-identical to the sequential pass; events of every kind interleaved in time order; floods sampled through a sparse padded field, which brought Mars from 147 to 50 seconds.
- **Outputs**: the sidecar lists scarps (with strike), the largest four thousand floods and their count, ridges, rises, shields, canyons, graben, coronae, domes, paterae, mountains, the lava area fraction, the tessera fraction and the resurfacing age; the exporter adds `units.png` and `surface_age.png`; the shaded preview is exposed to a common mean under a 30° sun.
- **Parameter graph**: 20 new nodes, documented in `docs/parameters.md` with formulas F35 to F37 and a revised F16.

Mars's finish grew from 50 to 77 seconds with the growth-aware ledger: every flood cell asks the slow processes what they added after it, through an index of the coarse cells each feature touches that also remembers which frames of a canyon reach the cell. Io's loop grew from under a second to seven with the steady-state surface laid at every kept step.

Mercury's smooth plains come to 17% against 27% on the real planet; the budget is right within the uncertainty of the extrusive share and the shut-off by contraction, which ends Mercury's volcanism 3.5 Gyr ago as observed. Mars's 160 craters above 300 km are what the lunar production function scaled by area and flux predicts; the real planet shows some 20 of them because its early erosion and the burial of the lowlands hid the rest, which waits for Phase 5.

## Findings during the phase

- **Lakes, not ponds.** The first flood model let lava pond in the nearest pit, spill and run on; on cratered ground it hopped through tiny pits until the hop limit and dropped most of its volume, and the chains of filled pits read as staircases. A single hydrostatic lake per eruption, grown from the nearest low in height order, fills a basin fed many times as one lake and keeps every cubic metre. A reach-limited version in between filled sub-regions of a basin to different levels and left the hummocks between them dry.
- **The ledger.** Refilling floods at the finish against the final coarse state fails when floods overlap, because the base carries every later flood's smear. Keeping a running sum of the smears already taken out, and leaving cells under younger craters to the base by the craters' fine footprints rather than the base's blurred ages, made overlapping floods compose exactly.
- **Restores and later surfaces.** A loop crater's fine restore used to rewrite its floor and ejecta units over cells that lava had covered later; the flood pass then took those cells for younger craters and left holes below the lava. The restore now leaves the surface of cells resurfaced after the crater alone.
- **Late giants.** The lunar production function extrapolated to the steady era put a 1,400 km basin on Mars 2.5 Gyr ago and 550 km craters on its shields. After the heavy bombardment the asteroid belt has no bodies left for such craters: the steady flux now falls two slopes steeper above 150 km and makes nothing over 600 km (F37).
- **Blocky margins.** Every coarse footprint refined at the finish needs a smooth outline, or its edge shows the loop's cells. Floods use the smeared indicator of their footprint at a threshold below a half with lobes of noise; troughs and calderas are carried at the loop's wall width and refined with the same width in the subtraction, or the residual ramp stays.
- **Contraction timing.** With contraction starting at formation the scarps formed under the heavy bombardment and were chopped to pieces; the onset and the cool-interior scaling moved the record to where the planets keep it.

## Review revisions

The review of 2026-10-11 raised the points below; each was taken up the same day and committed on its own.

- **Shields digging into the ground round them** (Mars seed 12 at 35.0, −69.1). A flood refilled at the finish took the final coarse ground as the ground it had found, so a dome, shield, canyon or corona that grew under it afterwards was undone at the lake's cells, which left moats and terraces round the shields. The slow processes' growth laws are now evaluated at any point and time (`stages/deform.rs`), through an index of the coarse cells each feature touches, and the flood and resurfacing passes take the growth since their own step off the base and put it back on what they write. The canyon's carving is part of it, so a lake older than the canyon is cut by it rather than filling it.
- **Sharp colour contrast of lava against its surroundings.** Every deposit now carries a coverage of the substrate in a new layer: lava by its thickness over 30 m, so a thin margin shows the ground through; craters in lava are lava through and through; the Io deposits moved to it from the melt field. On an airless surface the coverage is mixed sideways by impact gardening, 4 to 5 km across contacts 3.5 Gyr old (Li and Mustard 2000) and narrower on younger ones, so old mare margins soften in colour while fresh flows stay sharp. Mercury's plains colour itself is left for the colour pass.
- **Maria in small scattered blobs.** The vents drew on crust thickness and low ground alone, so every basin took a share. A new node, `volcanism.province_count`, gives magmatic provinces, one per 2,000 km of radius: the plume rises first, then random regions 40° wide with the rest of the body at 3% of their weight (the Procellarum terrane, Mercury's northern plains, Tharsis). The Moon's maria now lie 93% on one hemisphere, 24% of its area against under 2% of the other, as the real nearside and farside do; the same budget fills fewer basins deeper, so the mare area fell from 14.8 to 12.9%. The provinces are in the sidecar.
- **The canyon's braided look.** The width pulsed on a sine along a trace that wobbled on another. Each canyon system now has three en-echelon troughs beside the main one, offset to either side by about a width along a quarter to a half of its length, half to four-fifths as wide and deep, with plateau remnants between them as the chasmata of Valles Marineris have; the width swells and narrows by noise, the trace wanders by noise, and the ends taper to points.
- **Venus homogeneous, without continents or radiating fractures.** The tessera are now chosen on the height without the rises' domes, which are young dynamic topography rather than old thick crust, and stand as crustal plateaus 2 km above the plains with a sloped edge and broad folds under the fine fabric. Every rise wider than 2,000 km has a canyon system, and on an episodic lid it is a rift zone of three arms radiating from the rise, each as long as the rise is wide (Devana Chasma through Beta Regio; Ganis, Dali and Parga Chasmata from Atla); the rifts and the graben, now on every rise, come after the resurfacing event, which would otherwise have filled them. Three in ten coronae are novae with six to fourteen fractures radiating past their rims. The shaded preview exaggerates the slopes where nine tenths of the surface lies under 4°, up to six times, so Venus reads in it as a cratered world does; the factor is in the sidecar as `preview_exaggeration`, and the albedo and height maps are untouched.
- **Io's many red rings and the halos clipping each other.** One plume in eight now carries a ring, the first drawn the giant Pele-class one, 1,100 to 1,400 km across and mostly red, the rest 160 to 500 km and mostly frost. A ring lays its deposit wherever it is denser than what is there, so halos overlap instead of stopping at a hard edge.
- **Whether the features arise from the parameters.** They do: the regime, the thermal history, the body's size and the tidal flux decide which processes run and how strongly, and the seeds decide where. A Mars preset at the Earth's size would get three rises and three provinces on a thicker lid; the presets only pin the inputs the real bodies have.

Two defects came to light on the way. A finish-only trough (the graben) took the current ground as its reference while also keeping the difference from the base, which counted the finish's work twice and let overlapping fractures stack into spikes kilometres high; it now cuts from the base as a loop-carried trough does. And the loop draws its crater population per step with the step index as the random index, so the preview and standard qualities, with 30 and 90 steps, put their large craters in different places: the rise gate measured 0.39 of the rise height at preview against 0.71 at standard for that reason. The gate was made robust to it, and the sampling was then changed (below) so the preview predicts the export.

## Second review

The second look of 2026-10-11 asked about light strings in the maria, the jaggedness and length of Venus's rifts, the hard counts behind the canyon and the rings, and whether Io's look could arise from processes rather than paint. It also set a principle for the project: a feature's number should come from a budget and a distribution, its kind from a probability, and its colour from materials and processes, so that the parameters produce something like the real body most of the time rather than the preset reproducing it every time.

- **Light strings in the maria.** The coverage fade followed every buried crest within 30 m of a lake's surface. Gardening mixes to the regolith's depth, 4 to 5 m on the maria, so the coverage depth is 6 m, and what shows through is the substrate as it is, deep-sourced material included.
- **One rift population per rise** (`stages/plume.rs`, F38). The canyon with its three neighbours, the three arms and the sixty graben are gone. The uplift stretches the lithosphere; the extension across the rift zone, a few times the uplift (Valles Marineris some 30 km on Tharsis's 8 km), is a new node, `tectonics.rift_extension`, spent as a population of normal-fault troughs from N(>L) ∝ L^−2 with depth, width and heave scaling with length, half of them joining an existing trough as an en-echelon neighbour. Which are chasmata and which are graben falls out of the draw: Mars gets some forty troughs the loop carries and eleven thousand graben, each Venus rise one or two rifts up to 3,000 km and thousands of graben in swarms, so the rift zones read as segmented and braided at the base resolution. The texture of the fault scarps themselves, below a kilometre, waits for Phase 7.
- **Io as a steady state from processes** (`stages/io.rs`). The regime is laid down afresh at every kept step on the base terrain, with its full timeline, so every epoch has an Io of its own. Paterae have fault-bounded polygonal outlines and floors that age from black silicate to a sulfur coat; flow fields are compound, lobes of different ages from black to brown; mountains live a few hundred million years and are replaced. Plumes come in two classes drawn by probability, the gas-rich Pele-class one in twenty, and the ring radius is the ballistic range of the class's eruption speed in the body's gravity, so one or two red rings over a thousand kilometres across and a dozen frost rings a few hundred across are what usually comes out; the fallout of a quiet centre's last plume fades, red sulfur within a couple of years and frost over a century, longer in the cold. The colour is not painted: each cell carries what the eruptions left on it, fresh silicate, red sulfur and frost, and the materials stage lights those by the temperature the latitude's insolation gives, which puts frost where it survives the day, the red of irradiated sulfur where it is cold, and bare rock on steep mountain flanks. The grey blobs were the mountains, the dark ones the flow fields and the perfect brown circles the quiet patera floors; all three now have the shapes and colours their processes give them. The fine texture of the plains, layered scarps and the mottling within deposits remain for Phase 7's detail synthesis.

## Quality invariance

Every sampler in the loop drew per step with the step index as its random index, so the preview, with 30 steps, and the standard quality, with 90, placed their craters, scarps and eruptions differently, and the Io surface too. The loop now has 900 fixed time bins, a multiple of every step count, and the craters, scarps and eruptions are drawn per bin with the bin as the index; the Io surface takes the bin its step ends in, and its mountains are chains drawn from the seed alone. The loop threshold is taken on the standard coarse grid at every quality, so the preview carries the same loop population as the export and the finish restores the small ones. On Mars every one of the 167 craters over 300 km, and 484 of the 503 over 100 km, now sit at the same place with the same size at preview and standard quality, the rise and shields are the same, and the rise's crown stands the same 0.34 of its height above its foot at both; the differences left are the replayed craters below the loop threshold and the fine grid. A trough's gate takes the deepest standing trough, since a long rift that runs into the lowlands can be filled by later floods, as happened to the longest on Mars.

## Deferred from the Phase 3 list

- Lava channels and rilles: a few kilometres wide, below the standard finishing cell on Venus and Mars; Phase 7 detail synthesis.
- Stratovolcanoes belong to arcs, so to the mobile lid in Phase 6.
- Plains flow-field texture, lake levels across a pass inside one lake (the hydrostatic approximation of F36), and a more varied mare colour by titanium.
- The relaxation of early basins under the hot lithosphere, noted in Phase 2, now has the lithosphere history to draw on but is not yet applied.

## Notes for Phase 4

- Ice worlds get their own regimes; the flood process and the Io process are gated by composition and tidal flux and will not run on them. Cryovolcanism should reuse the lake fill with the cryolava material.
- The thermal history's relief limit follows the elastic thickness; for ice shells the elastic thickness node already uses the 170 K isotherm.
- Mercury's weathering timescales (0.15 Gyr from the inverse-square scaling) still want calibration when Mercury's colour is reviewed; space weathering now saturates in 0.3 Gyr at 1 AU.
